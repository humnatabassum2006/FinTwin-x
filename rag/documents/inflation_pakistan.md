# Inflation and Purchasing Power

## Why it matters
Inflation silently raises the expense side of a budget while incomes lag. A
household that spends 100% of income with 8% inflation loses roughly 8% of real
purchasing power per year unless income grows at the same pace.

## Mechanics in FinTwin-X
The Monte-Carlo engine draws a per-path annual inflation regime (default
mean 7.5%) and compounds it monthly. Because inflation is drawn per path, the
model produces a *distribution* of real outcomes rather than one number.

## Coping levers
1. Income growth at or above inflation (annual increment, skills, side income).
2. Hedged assets: equities, gold and real assets historically outpace inflation
   over long horizons, with high short-term volatility.
3. Expenditure substitution: switching brands, bulk buying, and reducing
   discretionary categories when the essential basket inflates fastest.
4. Fixed-rate debt: inflation erodes the real value of fixed EMIs, which is why
   long-tenor fixed-rate borrowing is less dangerous than it appears — but only
   if the payment is affordable today.

## Reading the scenario
If a +4pp inflation scenario drops your goal probability sharply, the binding
constraint is the surplus, not the return assumption. The fix is usually
contribution or income, not a riskier portfolio.
