# Budgeting Methodologies

## 50/30/20 rule
A simple allocation heuristic: 50% of after-tax income to needs (housing, food,
utilities, transport, minimum debt payments), 30% to wants, and 20% to savings
and debt repayment. In high-inflation economies the "needs" bucket often exceeds
50%, which is a signal to attack the largest fixed cost (usually housing) rather
than to cut the savings rate to zero.

## Zero-based budgeting
Every rupee of income is assigned a job before the month begins, so that
income minus expenses minus savings equals zero. It is the most effective method
for irregular income because each month is planned from scratch instead of
relying on last month's average.

## Envelope / cash-stuffing
Discretionary categories receive fixed envelopes; when an envelope is empty,
spending in that category stops. Digitally this is implemented as category-level
caps with alerts, which is exactly what FinTwin-X tracks through
`discretionary_ratio` and `spending_growth`.

## Pay-yourself-first
Savings move on payday, before any spending happens. Behaviourally this is the
highest-leverage change for a low savings-discipline score, because it converts
saving from a decision into a default.

## Pakistan-specific cash-flow notes
Salary cycles usually include two Eid bonuses and an annual increment. Treat
bonuses as capital (debt reduction, emergency fund, goal funding), not as
recurring income; otherwise lifestyle inflation absorbs them permanently.
Utility bills peak in summer, and education fees cluster in specific months, so
a flat monthly budget understates the peak months.
