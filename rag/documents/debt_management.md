# Debt Management

## Key ratios
- Debt-service ratio (DSR) = monthly debt payments ÷ monthly income.
  Below 0.20 is comfortable; 0.36 is the conventional lending ceiling; above
  0.50 is severe stress.
- Debt-to-income (DTI) = total outstanding balance ÷ annual income.
  Below 1.0 is generally manageable.
- Utilisation = outstanding ÷ original principal (loan age proxy).

## Repayment strategies
- **Avalanche**: pay minimums everywhere, direct all extra cash to the
  highest-interest balance. Mathematically optimal — minimises total interest.
- **Snowball**: clear the smallest balance first. Behaviourally superior for
  many people because early wins sustain motivation.
- **Refinancing / balance transfer**: rational when the new rate is lower than
  the old rate *after* fees, and the remaining term is long enough to recover
  the switching cost.

## Warning signs
Rising revolving balances, paying only the minimum, using new credit to service
old credit, or a DSR above 0.40 combined with less than one month of liquid
cover. In FinTwin-X these patterns raise the stress probability strongly because
they compound with income shocks.

## Islamic financing alternatives
Murabaha (cost-plus sale), Ijara (lease) and Diminishing Musharaka are
commonly used for vehicles and homes in Pakistan. Compare the total amount
payable rather than the quoted "profit rate" alone.
